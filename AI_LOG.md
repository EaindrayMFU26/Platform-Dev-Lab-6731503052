## 4. Decisions the AI made

These were the AI's choices, not requirements of the brief. Each one is explained in
`API_CONTRACT.md` ("Required behaviour and optional choices", "Assumptions") or `SCHEMA.md`.

- An unknown `equipmentId` returns 404 rather than 400.
- A booking may start exactly when another ends (the end time is exclusive).
- Date-times must include a time zone and are stored and returned in UTC.
- Bookings in the past are allowed; there is no minimum or maximum length.
- All five fields are required on create; text is trimmed; limits of 64, 100 and 500 characters.
- Booking ids are random UUIDs; unknown properties in a request body are ignored.
- The overlap check and the write are one SQL statement instead of a check followed by an insert.
- Times are stored as text with a `CHECK` on the format, not as numbers.
- The overlap rule is not also enforced by database triggers.
- CORS allows every origin.
- The names of the Worker (`equipment-booking-api`) and of the D1 database
  (`equipment-booking-db`). Cloudflare placed the database in its APAC region automatically.

## D. Decisions the AI made

These were the AI's choices, not requirements of the assignment. Where each is explained is in
brackets.

- The problem: routing support messages for the existing booking API, instead of a new project.
- The model: `@cf/cloudflare/clef` on Cloudflare Workers AI as the "equivalent decision model".
  No TypeSafe account or Jev API key was available in the session, while the Cloudflare login from
  the midterm already allowed Workers AI. The same request in Jev form is `docs/A6/jev-request.json`;
  Jev itself was never called. (Report, section 2)
- Four queues, three urgency levels and one yes/no question, and their wording. The wording was
  written once and not tuned against the test results. (Report, section 2)
- The policy: urgency is the most probable level; a person is needed when the yes/no probability is
  at least 0.5 or the confidence in the queue is below 0.6. Both thresholds were chosen by judgement,
  not measured. (Report, sections 2 and 4)
- The fallback: keyword rules in English and Thai; every fallback decision is flagged for a person
  and has confidence 0. On a tie an access problem beats a fault, which beats a booking change; the
  first version put the fault first (section E). (Report, section 3)
- An 8 second time limit. A timed-out model call is not cancelled, only ignored.
  (`src/support-router.ts`)
- `POST /support/route` does not look up `context.equipmentId`; `POST /support/tickets` does (404).
  `context.bookingId` is never looked up. (`API_CONTRACT.md`)
- Limits: 2000 characters per message, 16 KB per request body (413), control characters refused,
  the error text of the AI service cut to 200 characters. A fallback writes a warning to the server
  log without the message text. (`API_CONTRACT.md`)
- The decision is stored twice in `support_tickets`: as columns for filtering and as JSON for
  auditing. `booking_id` has no foreign key. (`SCHEMA.md`)
- The evidence was produced against the local server; the deployed Worker was not updated.
